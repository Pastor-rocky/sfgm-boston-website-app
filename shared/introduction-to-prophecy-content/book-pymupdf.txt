Lesson 1: Prophecies Concerning Jesus
INTRODUCTION Most people open their Bibles looking for comfort, instruction, or answers to life's 
questions. Few open it looking for a code. But the Bible is not a simple book. It is a library of 66 
texts, written over 1,500 years, by more than 40 authors across three continents, in three 
languages. And running through every page, every chapter, and every word is a single unbroken 
thread: Jesus Christ.
THE SCROLLS OF THE SYNAGOGUE 
In the ancient world, these 66 texts were not bound together. They were massive, heavy scrolls kept 
in the synagogue. When Jesus walked into the synagogue in Nazareth and unrolled the scroll of 
Isaiah, He was handling a sacred, hand copied document that had been passed down for 
generations. He read a prophecy about Himself, rolled it back up, and said that today this scripture 
is fulfilled in your hearing. That is the power of the text we are studying.
This course is about prophecy. Not the kind that feels distant or theoretical. The kind that stops you 
in your tracks. The kind that makes you set the book down and sit quietly for a moment because 
what you just read is impossible by any human standard, and yet it happened exactly as written.
Revelation 19:10 tells us that the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy. That means prophecy 
is not a side subject. It is not an advanced topic reserved for scholars or end times enthusiasts. It is 
the heartbeat of Scripture. To understand prophecy is to understand the Bible. To ignore it is to read

the Bible with one eye closed.
Here is what makes this study different from a typical Bible study. We are not going to ask you to

Open your Bible. We start at the very beginning.
1. WHERE DOES JESUS FIRST APPEAR?
Prophecy takes up 27 percent of the Bible. That means one in four verses is prophetic. A 2010 
survey by the American Bible Society found that fewer than half of regular churchgoers read their 
Bible daily, and prophecy ranks among the least taught subjects in Sunday services today. Yet the 
testimony of Jesus is, by Scripture's own definition, the spirit of prophecy.
How far into the Bible do we have to go before we find Jesus? Do we have to reach the Gospels?
The answer is no. Jesus appears on the very first page.
2. THE FIRST WORD
Genesis 1:1 "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth."
This is the Hebrew word "BERESHIT." Hebrew is unlike any other language. It operates on three 
levels at once. Letters function as pictures, those pictures carry numerical values, and the numbers 
tell deeper stories. Every word in the Hebrew Bible contains layers of meaning invisible in a standard 
English translation.
BERESHIT is the very first word in Scripture. On the surface, it describes creation. Beneath the 
surface, it tells the story of the cross.
DEEP DIVE INTO THE PICTOGRAPHS To truly understand how impossible this is, you must look at 
the six Hebrew letters that make up the word BERESHIT. They are Bet, Resh, Aleph, Shin, Yod, and 
Tav. In ancient Hebrew, Bet is a picture of a tent or house. Resh is a picture of a person's head, 
meaning the highest or the first. Aleph is an ox head, meaning God or a strong leader. Shin is a 
picture of teeth, meaning to consume or destroy. Yod is a picture of a hand or arm. 
Tav is a picture of two crossed sticks forming a cross, meaning a mark or covenant. Using the 
Hebrew pictographic method of translation, here is what it says:


---PAGE---

"The Son of God crowned with thorns upon His head on a cross, a gift of the covenant."
Another translation reads:
"The Son of God will lay down His life by His own hand." This is confirmed in John 10:18:
"No man taketh my life from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have the power to lay it down, and I 
have the power to take it again. This commandment have I received from my Father."
Consider what this means. Before God created light, before He formed the seas or the stars, the 
story of the cross was encoded into the first word He spoke. The deeper you look, the more you will 
find. Every word in the Bible carries significant meaning that points to Christ. Amen.
3. THE FIRST PROPHECY SPOKEN
Confirmation appears immediately after the fall of man in Genesis 3:15:
"I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and her offspring, he 
shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel."
God is speaking about Jesus here. This is the very first spoken prophecy in the Bible, and scholars 
call it the Protoevangelium, a Latin term meaning first gospel. It was delivered immediately after the 
first sin, before Adam and Eve had even left the garden.
THE CRUSHED HEEL Notice the exact phrasing God uses. He tells the serpent that the offspring of 
the woman will bruise his head, but the serpent will only bruise his heel. A bruised heel is painful, 
but it is a temporary injury. A crushed head is a fatal, permanent defeat. God was telling Satan right 
there in the garden that while the cross would cause Jesus temporary suffering, that same cross 
would be the weapon that permanently destroyed the power of sin and death.
God had told them not to eat from the tree in the middle of the garden, or they would surely die. 
They disobeyed. Sin entered the world. But God was not caught off guard. The answer was already 
written in the first word.
This also tells us something critical about the nature of God. He sees everything before it happens. 
He is all knowing. He will never need to ask what we should do now. He already knows. This is why 
prophecy matters. It is God telling us what He has already seen.
4. A HISTORICAL NOTE ON PROPHECY


---PAGE---

The writing of the Old Testament was completed approximately 400 years before the birth of Christ. 
The Dead Sea Scrolls, discovered between 1947 and 1956, confirmed that the prophetic texts we 
read today had not been altered or added to after the fact.
THE DISCOVERY IN THE CAVE The story of how these scrolls were found is a modern miracle. In the 
spring of 1947, a young Bedouin shepherd boy was watching his flock near the ancient ruins of 
Qumran, near the Dead Sea. One of his goats wandered off. He threw a rock into a dark cave, 
hoping to scare the animal out. Instead of a bleating goat, he heard the sound of shattering pottery. 
Inside that cave were clay jars containing ancient scrolls that had been perfectly preserved in the 
dry desert climate for two thousand years.
Among them was the complete scroll of Isaiah. When scholars translated it, they found that it 
matched our modern Bible with breathtaking accuracy. God protected His prophetic word in clay 
jars in the desert for two millennia just to prove to our modern generation that these prophecies 
were written long before Jesus was born.
Scholars verified that the scrolls, some dating to 150 BC, contained the same prophecies we read 
now. This removes any argument that the prophecies were written after Jesus lived. They were 
written centuries before He was born, and He fulfilled every one of them.
5. TWO LANDMARK PROPHECIES
Psalm 22 is one of the strongest examples of messianic prophecy in the entire Bible. Written by 
King David around 1000 BC, it records the words of a man being crucified in precise detail, including 
the piercing of hands and feet, the casting of lots for clothing, bones out of joint, and the mocking 
of bystanders.
THE HORRORS OF ROMAN CRUCIFIXION To grasp the miracle of Psalm 22, you must understand 
the history of crucifixion. The Persians originally invented this method of execution, but the Romans 
perfected it to maximize human agony. It was designed to cause death by slow suffocation. When 
David wrote about the piercing of hands and feet, the intense thirst, and the bones being pulled out 
of joint, he was describing the exact medical realities of Roman crucifixion. Yet David lived in a time 
when the standard Jewish method of execution was stoning. He had absolutely no earthly frame of 
reference for the terrifying death he was describing. The Holy Spirit gave him the exact image of the 
cross centuries before the cross was ever built.
Crucifixion as a method of execution did not exist in David's time. The Romans did not adopt it until 
roughly 600 years later. David described something he had never seen and could not have 
imagined, yet every detail matches the account of the crucifixion.

7. THE MATHEMATICS OF PROPHECY
There are over 300 prophecies concerning where Jesus would be born, how He would die, and His 
resurrection. In 1969, mathematician and professor Peter Stoner published a study in his book 
"Science Speaks," in which he calculated the probability of one person fulfilling just 8 of the 300 
prophecies by chance. His figure was 1 in 10 to the 17th power. That is 1 in 
100,000,000,000,000,000.
To illustrate that number, Stoner described covering the entire state of Texas two feet deep in silver 
dollars, marking one of them, and then asking a blindfolded person to reach into that sea of coins 
and pull out the marked one on the first try.
THE SILVER DOLLAR EXPERIMENT Let us visualize exactly what Peter Stoner was describing. The 
state of Texas is over 268,000 square miles. If you covered that entire landmass in silver dollars, the 
coins would reach two feet high. That is an ocean of silver. Now, take just one silver dollar, paint a 
red mark on it, and drop it anywhere into that massive pile from an airplane. Then blindfold a man. 
Tell him he can travel as far as he wants, but he must reach down into the two feet of coins and pull 
out the marked silver dollar on his very first try. The odds of him doing that are the exact same odds 
of Jesus fulfilling just eight prophecies by chance. And Jesus did not fulfill eight. He fulfilled all 300.
Jesus fulfilled all 300. He did not do it by chance. He did it because He is exactly who the Bible says 
He is.

Lesson 2: Replacement Theology
Every doctrine has a history. Most people sitting in church on Sunday morning have never asked 
where their beliefs came from, who shaped them, or whether those beliefs line up with what the 
Bible actually says. That question matters more than most realize, because the doctrine we examine 
in this lesson has quietly shaped the way the entire Western church reads Scripture for nearly 2,000 
years.
Replacement theology sounds like an academic term. It is not. It is a belief that touches every part 
of how you read your Bible. It affects which prophecies you apply to yourself, which ones you skip 
over, and whether the Old Testament promises still have a future or have already been reassigned. 
Most people who hold this belief have never chosen it. It was handed to them.
This lesson is not about pointing fingers at denominations or movements. It is about reading the 
Bible honestly. If God said something to Israel, it belongs to Israel. If He said something to the 
church, it belongs to the church. Mixing those two up does not produce deeper truth. It produces 
confusion. And confusion about prophecy is exactly what replacement theology guarantees.
By the end of this lesson, you will understand what this doctrine is, where it came from, what 
Scripture says in response to it, and why getting this right is one of the most important steps you 
can take toward understanding the rest of this course.
THE MIRACLE OF MAY 1948
For nearly two millennia, theologians who taught Replacement Theology had one major historical 
argument on their side. Israel did not exist as a nation. It seemed entirely logical to assume God was 
finished with them. But on May 14, 1948, the greatest prophetic miracle of the modern era took 
place. The Jewish people, after being scattered across the globe for nearly two thousand years, 
were reestablished as a sovereign nation in their ancient homeland in a single day.
This fulfilled the exact prophecy of Isaiah 66:8 which asks if a country can be born in a day or a 
nation be brought forth in a moment. When Israel raised its flag in 1948, it shattered the 


---PAGE---

foundational argument of Replacement Theology. God was not finished with Israel. He was just 
waiting for the appointed time.
1. WHAT IS REPLACEMENT THEOLOGY?
Replacement theology is the belief that any time Israel appears in the Bible, it should be replaced 
with the church. Under this view, God has permanently set aside the nation of Israel, and all 
promises made to Israel now belong exclusively to the church.
This belief is common and spreads easily, but it is not true. In my view, this is the most damaging 
doctrine in the church today. It distorts the truth of the Bible by removing proper interpretation from 
the equation. The technical term for sound biblical interpretation is hermeneutics.
WORD STUDY: HERMENEUTICS
The word hermeneutics comes from the Greek root hermeneuo, which means to translate or 
interpret. In the ancient world, it was connected to Hermes, the mythological messenger who was 
responsible for delivering and translating the messages of the gods to humanity. In biblical study, 
hermeneutics is the strict science of drawing the actual meaning out of a text rather than reading 
our own opinions into it. When we abandon hermeneutics, we start making the Bible say whatever 
we want it to say.
Replacement theology abandons that standard. Whenever a passage addresses Israel, this doctrine 
causes people to apply it to the church instead, stripping the text of its original context and 
intended meaning.
This creates serious error. If a message was intended for Israel and we apply it to ourselves, we 
misread the Bible entirely. You may think you do not hold this belief, but the truth is, it has been 
quietly embedded in Christian teaching since the early centuries of the church.
2. A HISTORICAL ORIGIN
Replacement theology did not begin as a fringe idea. It gained significant traction in the early 
church through the writings of theologians such as Origen from AD 185 to 254 and later Augustine 
of Hippo from AD 354 to 430, who argued that the church had inherited the promises given to 
Israel.
By the time of the Council of Nicaea in AD 325, much of the church had already begun distancing 
itself from Jewish roots and reinterpreting Old Testament promises as belonging to the church.
INSIDE THE COUNCIL OF NICAEA


---PAGE---

The Council of Nicaea in AD 325 was a massive turning point. Emperor Constantine had recently 
legalized Christianity and he wanted to unify his empire. But Constantine held a deep political and 
cultural prejudice against the Jewish people. During this council, he actively pushed the church to 
separate entirely from its Jewish roots.
One of the most devastating decisions made at Nicaea was legally separating the Christian 
celebration of the resurrection from the Jewish feast of Passover. Constantine declared that the 
church should have nothing in common with the Jewish people. This political decision paved the 
way for the church to see itself as a complete replacement for Israel rather than a wild branch 
grafted into the Jewish olive tree.
This shift had real world consequences. Throughout the Middle Ages, the belief that God had 
permanently rejected the Jewish people was used to justify persecution, forced conversions, and 
exclusion. The theology itself shaped centuries of history.
When Martin Luther launched the Reformation in 1517, he initially sought dialogue with Jewish 
communities, hoping they would receive the gospel. When they did not, he turned sharply against 
them, writing texts that reflected the same replacement thinking embedded in Catholic theology 
before him. The doctrine ran deep on both sides of the Reformation divide.
LUTHER'S TRAGIC SHIFT
Martin Luther's story is a tragic example of how deeply this error was rooted. In 1523, Luther wrote 
a beautiful essay titled That Jesus Christ Was Born a Jew, urging Christians to treat the Jewish 
people with love so they might be won to the gospel. But twenty years later, when the Jewish 
communities still did not convert in large numbers, Luther grew bitter. Because he carried the 
underlying assumption that the church had replaced Israel, he could not understand their continued 
existence. 
In 1543, he published a vicious pamphlet titled On the Jews and Their Lies (Von den Juden und 
ihren Lügen), in which he called for synagogues to be burned, Jewish homes destroyed, their books 
confiscated, and the Jewish people stripped of protection and driven into forced labor or exile. 
These were not passing words—they were a 65,000-word treatise that would echo through 
European history with devastating consequences. Bad theology always produces bad fruit.
Hitler is the greatest example. Claiming the name of Christianity, he got his hands on Luther's 
writings—particularly On the Jews and Their Lies—and used them to help shape and justify Nazi 
ideology. The Nazis displayed Luther's pamphlet in a glass case at the Nuremberg rallies, and Julius 
Streicher, publisher of the Nazi propaganda paper Der Stürmer, even cited Luther in his defense at 


---PAGE---

the Nuremberg Trials, arguing he had only repeated what Luther wrote centuries earlier. Some 
historians note that Kristallnacht (November 9–10, 1938)—when synagogues across Germany were 
burned—took place near Luther's birthday, and Nazi leaders framed it as the fulfillment of Luther's 
recommendations. What began as a theological error about Israel ended in the ashes of the 
Holocaust. Bad theology is never just bad theology—it eventually costs lives.
Understanding this history is not meant to condemn the reformers or the early church fathers. It is 
meant to show how a theological error, once embedded, shapes everything that follows.
3. THE STATISTICS
Consider these numbers. In America, 80 percent of denominations hold to replacement theology. 
Only 20 percent of churches in America regularly teach prophecy. A 2020 study by Life-way 
Research found that only 6 percent of Americans hold a biblical worldview, meaning they interpret 
Scripture as a whole, in context, without substituting their own cultural assumptions.
It is reasonable to ask whether the 80 percent that hold to replacement theology and the 80 
percent that do not teach prophecy are, in large part, the same group. Replacement theology and 
the absence of prophetic teaching are not a coincidence. They are connected. Most prophecy in the 
Bible is addressed specifically to Israel. If you replace Israel with the church in every passage, the 
prophetic framework collapses. The two go together.
4. HAS GOD REJECTED ISRAEL?
Romans 11:1 records Paul the Apostle asking directly: "I ask then, has God rejected his people? By 
no means! For I myself am an Israelite, a descendant of Abraham, a member of the tribe of 
Benjamin."
Paul wrote this around AD 57, roughly 25 years after the resurrection of Christ. The church was 
already growing rapidly. Gentile believers were entering by the thousands. And yet Paul, a Jewish 
man, a trained Pharisee, and one of the most educated theologians of his age, felt it necessary to 
state plainly that God has not rejected Israel.
We must ask the same question today. Has God rejected the nation of Israel? The same chapter 
answers clearly, no.
Romans 11:23 tells us God is able to graft Israel back into the family tree. Romans 11:18 tells us God 
grafted the church, the Gentiles, into that same tree. It also carries a direct warning not to be 
arrogant toward the branches that were cut off. It is not you who support the root, but the root that 
supports you. The root is Jesus. Romans 11:11 tells us God will provoke Israel to jealousy, and 


---PAGE---

through that jealousy, Israel will find Christ.
DEEP DIVE: THE OLIVE TREE METAPHOR
In this chapter, Paul uses the brilliant metaphor of an olive tree to explain the relationship between 
Israel and the church. In ancient agriculture, if an olive tree stopped producing fruit, a farmer would 
cut off the dead branches and graft in wild olive branches. The wild branches would then draw life 
from the rich, established root system of the original tree.
Paul tells the Gentile church that they are the wild branches. They have been grafted into the 
covenants and promises of Israel. But he warns them not to boast against the original branches that 
were cut off, because God has the power to graft the natural branches right back into their own 
tree. The tree was never chopped down. The root is still holy.
In Matthew 23:39, Jesus speaks directly to the nation of Israel: "You will not see me again until you 
learn to cry, 'Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord.'"
This is a future event. Jesus told Israel they would see Him again, but only after they call on His 
name. That has not fully happened yet. It is still ahead. Replacement theology has no room for this 
passage because it requires a future for the nation of Israel, not a replacement.
5. WHO CAN ENTER THE FAMILY TREE?
John 1:12 says: "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to 
become children of God."
The door is open to everyone. To be part of the family tree, belief in Jesus as the Christ is required. 
For Gentiles, the path is straightforward. For the nation of Israel, that path carries a weight and a 
history unlike any other. Their coming to faith is not a small event. According to Romans 11, it is 
connected to the fullness of the Gentiles and to the return of Christ.
6. THE BIRTH OF THE CHURCH
The first mention of the word church appears in Matthew 16, beginning at verse 13. Jesus asks His 
disciples who do men say that I am. They answered that some said He was a prophet, others Elijah. 
Then Jesus asked who do you say I am. Peter answered, "You are the Christ, the Son of the living 
God."
Jesus responded: "Blessed are you, Peter, for flesh and blood has not revealed this to you but my 
Father in heaven. And I tell you, upon this rock, I will lay the foundation of my church."


---PAGE---

The rock was not Peter. Peter was one of the strongest disciples, but Jesus was referring to the 
confession Peter made that Jesus is the Christ. That confession is the foundation on which the 
church is built. The Greek word used here for church is ekklesia, meaning called out ones. The 
church is not a building. It is a people, called out of the world, built on the truth of who Jesus is.
WORD STUDY: EKKLESIA
The Greek word ekklesia is a combination of two words. Ek meaning out of, and kaleo meaning to 
call. Together, they mean the called out ones. But in the first century Roman world, an ekklesia was 
not a religious term. It was a political one. An ekklesia was an assembly of citizens called out of their 
homes by the town herald to govern the affairs of the city.
When Jesus chose this specific word to describe His followers, He was making a profound

3. UNDERSTANDING THE WEEKS

The word translated weeks in this passage is the Hebrew word shabuim, which means a period of 
seven. Think of it as a week of years rather than days, meaning each week equals seven years. 
Seventy weeks therefore equals 490 years.
WORD STUDY: SHABUIM AND THE CYCLE OF SEVENS
When Western readers see the word weeks, they naturally think of a seven day calendar. But the 
Hebrew mind operated on a divine rhythm of sevens. The word shabuim literally means a heptad, or 
a unit of seven. Just as the Jewish people observed a Sabbath day of rest every seven days, God 
commanded them to observe a Sabbath year every seven years, allowing the agricultural land to 
rest. Gabriel is speaking in this exact prophetic language. He is telling Daniel that God has 
appointed seventy sets of seven years to finish His redemptive work for the Jewish nation.
Gabriel tells Daniel that 490 years have been decreed for the nation of Israel and the city of 
Jerusalem to accomplish six specific things:
To finish the transgression
To make an end of sins
To make reconciliation for iniquity
To bring in everlasting righteousness
To seal up the vision and prophecy
To anoint the Most Holy
These are not spiritual metaphors applied to the church. This is a specific, national, covenantal 
declaration addressed to Israel and Jerusalem. Gabriel said so plainly, thy people and thy holy city.
4. THE TIMELINE
Gabriel gives a starting point. The going forth of the commandment to restore and rebuild 
Jerusalem. This is historically verifiable. The decree was given by the Persian king Artaxerxes I in 
445 BC. This is recorded in Nehemiah 2:1 through 8.
From that decree, Gabriel said:
Seven weeks, plus sixty two weeks, equals sixty nine weeks, or 483 years
At the end of those 483 years, the Messiah would arrive
DEEP DIVE: SIR ROBERT ANDERSON AND THE PROPHETIC YEAR
How did scholars arrive at this exact date? They had to understand God's calendar. The ancient 
biblical calendar, the one used in Genesis and Revelation, operates on a 360 day prophetic lunar 
year, not our modern 365 day solar year. If you take 69 weeks of years, which is exactly 483 years, 
and multiply it by 360 days, you get exactly 173,880 days. When you calculate 173,880 days from 


---PAGE---

the Persian decree issued on March 14, 445 BC, and adjust for leap years, you land perfectly on 
April 6, AD 32.
Sir Robert Anderson, in his 1894 book "The Coming Prince," calculated this timeline using the 
Jewish prophetic calendar of 360 day years. His calculation placed the end of the 69th week on 
April 6, AD 32, the precise date of the triumphal entry of Jesus into Jerusalem, the very day He rode 
in on a donkey and the crowds shouted Hosanna.
This is one of the most staggering calculations in the study of prophecy. Written over 500 years 
before Jesus was born, Daniel 9 predicted the day of His arrival in Jerusalem. Not the year. Not the 
decade. The day.
5. THE GAP: THE CHURCH AGE
After the 69th week, Gabriel states that the Messiah would be cut off. This refers to the crucifixion. 
He also says that the people of a prince who shall come would destroy the city and the sanctuary. 
This was fulfilled in AD 70 when the Roman general Titus destroyed Jerusalem and the temple, 
killing over a million Jewish people. The Jewish historian Josephus documented this event in detail.
HISTORICAL CONTEXT: THE DESTRUCTION OF THE TEMPLE IN AD 70
The precision of Daniel's prophecy regarding the destruction of the sanctuary is terrifying. In AD 70, 
the Roman legions under General Titus surrounded Jerusalem and starved the city. During the final 
siege, a massive fire broke out in the temple. The heat was so intense that the massive gold 
decorations on the temple walls and ceiling melted and seeped deep into the cracks of the stone 
floor. To recover the melted gold, the Roman soldiers literally used iron pry bars to flip over and 
smash every single massive stone block. This completely leveled the sanctuary, perfectly fulfilling 
the words of Jesus when He said not one stone would be left upon another.
Then the prophecy pauses. There are 69 weeks accounted for, and one week remaining, but the 
70th week has not yet occurred. This gap between the 69th and 70th week is what we are living in 
right now. It is the church age. The clock stopped when Israel rejected the Messiah, and it will 
restart when God turns His attention back to the nation of Israel in the final seven year period.
This is why understanding replacement theology matters so much. If you replace Israel with the 
church, the 70th week has no meaningful recipient. The prophecy loses its covenantal target. It 
becomes impossible to interpret correctly.

Leviticus 23 verses 4 through 5 says that these are the Lord's appointed festivals, the official days

CULTURAL CONNECTION: THE HIDDEN AFIKOMEN. If you attend a traditional Jewish Passover

The leader takes the middle piece of bread, breaks it in half, wraps one half in white linen, and hides

house to find the hidden bread. When it is brought back, a ransom price is paid for it, and everyone 
eats from it.
The three pieces of bread represent the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. It is the middle piece,

Leviticus 23 verse 6 says that on the next day, the 15th day of the month, you must begin

leaven symbolized a life lived without sin.
DEEP DIVE: SWEEPING THE HOUSE. The Jewish preparation for this feast is incredibly profound.

feather. He walks through the dark house using the light of the candle to find any hidden crumbs of 
leaven. When he finds the sin, he uses the feather to sweep it onto the wooden spoon. Then, the 
wooden spoon and the leaven are taken outside and burned in a fire.
Prophetically, the candle represents the Word of God, which lights up the dark places of our hearts. 
The feather represents the Holy Spirit, sweeping our sins away. And the wooden spoon represents 
the wooden cross, where all of our sins were placed and judged by the consuming fire of God's 
wrath. Jesus was buried on the 15th, the very day this feast began.

without sin. As bread is baked without leaven, so the body of Christ was laid in the earth without a 
single trace of sin. He fulfilled this feast on the exact day it was appointed.

3. SEEKING THE BRIDE AND THE WATER TEST When it is time to find a daughter in law, the 
process usually begins within the family. This is 100 percent a Jewish custom, and more 
importantly, a biblical principle. When Abraham wanted a wife for his son Isaac, he told his most 
trusted servant to go to his country and his own relatives and get a wife for his son Isaac.

reveal the chosen young woman through an offer of water. He prayed that when he asked a young 
woman to let down her jar so he could have a drink, and she offered to water his camels too, she 
would be the one God had chosen.
In our culture, we have a very similar custom. When we want to know if a girl wants to come into our 
family, we ask her to pour us some water. If she comes back with a glass full, we know she wants to 
come. If she comes back with a glass half empty, we know we need to go somewhere else.
When Rebekah not only gave Abraham's servant water but offered to water his camels, he knew he 
had found the one. The most beautiful part happened when her family asked her if she would go 
with this man. She did not hesitate or negotiate conditions. She simply said, "I will go."
Her willing acceptance is a prophetic foreshadowing of the sinner's prayer. God does not force us 
into a relationship. He waits for our response, and when we accept Him willingly, Jesus gives us life 
giving water. Jesus said He is the life giving water, and anyone who thirsts should come after Him 
and drink.
4. NEGOTIATING THE PRICE AND THE CONTRACT (THE KETUBAH) Once we know she is the one,

In the time of Jesus, the groom would pick his bride based on reputation, tell his father, and they

This contract contained a very specific stipulation regarding time. For example, if the contract was

waiting for was the bride to agree.

witnesses to this contract were the Father and the Holy Spirit.

CULTURAL CONNECTION: THE MIDNIGHT CRY In the ancient Middle East, the groom would 
almost always come for his bride at midnight. The streets of the city would be completely dark and 
silent. Suddenly, a designated herald would run ahead of the wedding procession, carrying a blazing 
torch and shouting at the top of his lungs to announce that the bridegroom was coming and they 
must go out to meet him. This shout was designed to startle the sleeping city and give the bride a 
few brief moments to light her lamp and step out the door. The Apostle Paul tells us that Jesus will 
descend with a shout and the voice of the archangel. The trumpet is the ultimate midnight cry, 
breaking into the darkness of this world to call the Bride home.
